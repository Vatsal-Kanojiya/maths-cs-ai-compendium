Lags, Tails, Qubits

Exponential smoothing of a price series and the membrane of a spiking neuron obey the same difference equation, and it is the one you wrote for a thermocouple. A minimum-variance portfolio is the beamformer of Sheet 09. Value at risk fails the way a series reliability calculation does. And a qubit, restricted to real amplitudes, has a Mohr’s circle.

!Read this first: the source chapters are outlines

Chapters 19 and 20 of the compendium are not written. Of their ten files, six are empty: drug discovery, agentic systems and healthcare in Chapter 19; datacentres in space, decentralised AI and brain–machine interfaces in Chapter 20. The other four hold a heading and eight or nine one-line bullets each, 33 bullets in all, naming topics without explaining any of them.

So this sheet is a coda, not a survey. A survey of AlphaFold, trading systems or neuromorphic chips would have to be built from claims this series cannot compute, and every sheet so far has refused to do that. Instead the coda picks six bullets whose mathematical core can be derived here and lands on your training, derives each one, checks every number in verify_claims.py, and names everything else in §09 without pretending to teach it. Anything not derived on this page is marked reported.

Nine of the 33 bullets are touched, six of them properly. That is a small fraction, and it is the right one: each of the six turned out to be something you have already computed under another name, and most of the correspondences are identities rather than likenesses. The price of the method is §09, which is long.

The headline is a repetition. The first-order lag \(x_{k} = (1-\alpha)\,x_{k-1} + \alpha\,u_k\) is exponential smoothing in Chapter 19 (§01) and the sampled membrane of a leaky integrate-and-fire neuron in Chapter 20 (§06), where \(\alpha = 1 - e^{-\Delta t/\tau}\) exactly. Sheet 08 found that the same mathematics turning up twice under two names is the strongest signal a chapter can give. Here it turns up in two chapters, in finance and in neuroscience, and both times it is the equation of a thermocouple in a furnace.

The best single result is also in §01: the smoothing constant that minimises forecast error is not a matter of taste. It is the steady-state Kalman gain of Sheet 11, the first-order sibling of that sheet’s 0.707 servo. The most useful warning is in §07: value at risk, the number many risk reports lead with, calls splitting an order between two suppliers riskier than single-sourcing it.
